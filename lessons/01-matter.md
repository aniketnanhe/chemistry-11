# Lesson 01: Matter

## Learning Objectives

By the end of this lesson, you should be able to:

- Define matter.
- Distinguish matter from non-matter.
- Explain why mass and volume are the defining properties of matter.
- Identify matter in everyday life.
- Avoid common misconceptions.

---

## Why This Topic Matters

Chemistry is the study of matter and the changes it undergoes.

Before learning about atoms, molecules, chemical reactions, or the periodic table, we must first answer a simple question:

**What exactly is matter?**

Everything else in chemistry builds upon this idea.

---

## Definition

Matter is **anything that has mass and occupies space.**

Both conditions are necessary.

---

## Building the Intuition

The chair you're sitting on, the water in your bottle, the air you're breathing, your phone, and your body all have something in common.

They all have mass.
They all occupy space.

Chemists call everything with these two properties **matter**.

Even invisible substances such as air are matter. Inflate a balloon and it becomes slightly heavier because the air inside has mass.

---

## Key Concepts

- Matter has mass.
- Matter occupies space (volume).
- Matter is made of atoms and molecules.
- Matter exists as solids, liquids and gases.

---

## Examples and Counter-examples

### Matter
- Air
- Water
- Ice
- Steam
- Smoke
- Wood
- Iron
- Gold
- Milk
- Petrol
- Cooking oil
- Oxygen
- Carbon dioxide
- Clouds
- Dust

### Not Matter
- Light
- Heat
- Sound
- Gravity
- Magnetism
- Time
- Thoughts

### Interesting Examples

**Perfume** — Matter. Tiny perfume molecules travel through the air.

**Cloud** — Matter. Made of water droplets or ice crystals.

**Smoke** — Matter. Contains gases and tiny solid particles.

---

## Common Misconceptions

- Air is matter.
- Heat is energy, not matter.
- Light is energy, not matter.
- Invisible does not mean non-existent.
