# 1. Atomic Mass

## Motivation

Atoms are far too small to weigh directly in kilograms or grams.
Chemists therefore compare the masses of atoms relative to a standard.

## Definition

-   **1 atomic mass unit (1 u)** is defined as **1/12 of the mass of one
    Carbon-12 atom**.
-   Carbon-12 is assigned **exactly 12 u**.

## Key Ideas

-   Atomic mass is a **relative** quantity.
-   Most atomic masses are close to integers because nuclei are built
    from protons and neutrons, each having masses close to 1 u.
-   They are not exact integers because of:
    -   Nuclear binding energy (mass defect)
    -   Isotopes

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# Curiosity Box

## Why are atomic masses close to integers?

This is partly due to our choice of unit (Carbon-12 = 12 u), but mainly
because nature builds nuclei from protons and neutrons whose masses are
both close to 1 u.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 2. Average Atomic Mass

## Motivation

Natural samples contain mixtures of isotopes.

Example: - Chlorine exists mainly as Cl-35 and Cl-37.

Therefore, the periodic table cannot list a single isotope's mass.

## Definition

Average atomic mass is the **weighted average** of the masses of
naturally occurring isotopes.

## Formula

Average Atomic Mass

= Σ(mass × abundance)/100

## Important Points

-   No individual atom has the average mass.
-   Average atomic mass belongs to the **element**, not to an individual
    atom.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 3. Molecular Mass

## Definition

The molecular mass is the **sum of the average atomic masses of all
atoms present in one molecule**.

Examples

H₂O

2×1 +16 =18 u

CO₂

12 +2×16 =44 u

CH₄

12 +4×1 =16 u

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 4. Formula Mass

## Motivation

Ionic compounds do not exist as discrete molecules.

NaCl is an ionic lattice, not a collection of NaCl molecules.

## Definition

Formula mass is the sum of the atomic masses represented in one formula
unit.

Example

NaCl

23 +35.5 =58.5 u

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 5. Mole Concept

## Motivation

Chemists cannot count atoms individually.

A mole is therefore a counting unit.

Just as

-   dozen =12 objects

Similarly

-   mole =6.02214076×10²³ entities

This number is Avogadro's constant.

## Key Idea

A mole tells **how many**, not **what**.

Examples

-   1 mole atoms
-   1 mole molecules
-   1 mole ions
-   1 mole electrons

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 6. Molar Mass

## Definition

Mass of one mole of a substance.

Unit

g/mol

## Beautiful Observation

  One Particle   One Mole
  -------------- ----------
  12 u           12 g
  16 u           16 g
  18 u           18 g
  44 u           44 g

The numerical value stays the same.

Only the unit changes.

# 8. Problem Solving Framework

Every numerical problem should begin with four questions.

1.  What is given?
2.  What is required?
3.  Convert through **moles**.
4.  What exactly am I counting?
    -   atoms
    -   molecules
    -   ions
    -   formula units

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 9. Common JEE Traps

-   Mole is **not** a unit of mass.
-   Distinguish atoms from molecules.
-   Distinguish molecular mass from formula mass.
-   Average atomic mass is not the mass of an individual atom.
-   Count atoms carefully in polyatomic molecules.
-   For gases, distinguish molecules from atoms (e.g. O₂).

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 10. Memorable Takeaways

-   Chemistry lets us **count atoms by weighing them**.
-   The mole is the bridge between the microscopic and macroscopic
    worlds.
-   Never memorize extra formulae---derive them through the mole bridge.
-   Before every calculation, ask: **"What exactly am I counting?"**
