# Lesson 08: Mass and Weight

> **Estimated Review Time:** 10–15 minutes

# Learning Objectives

- Distinguish between mass and weight.
- Understand how gravity affects weight.
- Use the relation W = mg.

# Why This Topic Matters

People often use the words *mass* and *weight* interchangeably, but in science they have different meanings. Understanding this difference prevents many later misconceptions.

# Building the Intuition

Take a 5 kg object to the Moon.

Its **mass** remains 5 kg because the amount of matter has not changed.

Its **weight** decreases because the Moon's gravity is weaker.

# Mass

Mass is the amount of matter in an object.

Characteristics:
- Constant everywhere.
- SI unit: kilogram (kg).
- Measured using a balance.

# Weight

Weight is the gravitational force acting on an object.

Formula:

W = mg

where:
- W = weight
- m = mass
- g = acceleration due to gravity

SI unit: newton (N).

# Examples

- A 10 kg object has the same mass on Earth and the Moon.
- Its weight is smaller on the Moon because g is smaller.

# Comparison

| Mass | Weight |
|---|---|
| Amount of matter | Gravitational force |
| Constant | Depends on gravity |
| kg | N |

# Common Misconceptions

- Mass and weight are not the same.
- Bathroom scales are calibrated to display mass, even though they measure weight.

# Think About It

1. Why do astronauts appear lighter on the Moon?
2. Can an object have mass but zero weight?

# Summary

Mass measures matter. Weight measures gravitational force.
