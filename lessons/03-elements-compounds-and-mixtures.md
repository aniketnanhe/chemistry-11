# Lesson 03: Elements, Compounds and Mixtures

> **Estimated Review Time:** 10–15 minutes

# Learning Objectives

- Define an element, compound, and mixture.
- Distinguish between them using composition and properties.
- Identify examples from everyday life.
- Explain why compounds cannot be separated by physical methods.
- Explain why mixtures can usually be separated physically.
- Recognize common misconceptions.

# Why This Topic Matters

In the previous lesson, we learned that matter can be classified into **pure substances** and **mixtures**.

But what exactly is a pure substance?

- Is gold pure?
- Is water pure?
- Is air pure?
- Is milk pure?
- Is steel pure?

To answer these questions, we first need to understand **elements, compounds, and mixtures**.

This lesson forms the foundation of almost every topic in chemistry.

# Building the Intuition

Imagine you have a box of LEGO bricks.

You can think of **atoms** as the individual LEGO pieces.

Sometimes all the pieces are identical.

Sometimes different pieces are permanently joined together.

Sometimes different pieces are simply mixed in the same box.

These three situations correspond to:

- Element
- Compound
- Mixture

# Elements

An **element** is a pure substance made of only **one type of atom**.

Examples:
- Hydrogen (H)
- Oxygen (O)
- Gold (Au)
- Copper (Cu)

**O₂ is still an element** because it contains only oxygen atoms.

# Compounds

A **compound** is a pure substance formed when **two or more different elements chemically combine in a fixed ratio**.

Examples:
- Water (H₂O)
- Carbon dioxide (CO₂)
- Sodium chloride (NaCl)

Compounds cannot usually be separated by physical methods because their atoms are chemically bonded.

# Mixtures

A **mixture** is formed when two or more substances are physically combined without chemical bonding.

Examples:
- Air
- Milk
- Brass
- Soil

Mixtures usually retain the properties of their components and can often be separated by physical methods.

# Common Misconceptions

- Water is **not** an element.
- Air is **not** a compound.
- O₂ is an element, not a compound.
- Brass is a mixture, not a compound.

# Summary

- Element → one kind of atom.
- Compound → chemically combined elements in a fixed ratio.
- Mixture → physically combined substances.
