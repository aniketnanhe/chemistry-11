# Lesson 10: Significant Figures

> **Estimated Review Time:** 10–15 minutes

# Learning Objectives
- Count significant figures correctly.
- Apply rounding rules.
- Understand why significant figures matter.

# Why This Topic Matters

Measurements are never perfectly exact. Significant figures communicate the precision of measured values.

# Building the Intuition

Writing 12.300 cm tells us more than writing 12.3 cm because the extra digits reflect measurement precision.

# Rules

- All non-zero digits are significant.
- Zeros between non-zero digits are significant.
- Leading zeros are not significant.
- Trailing zeros after a decimal point are significant.

# Examples

- 45.6 → 3 SF
- 0.00450 → 3 SF
- 1002 → 4 SF
- 1200 (without decimal) → ambiguous unless specified.

# Common Misconceptions

- All zeros are not significant.
- More digits do not always mean greater accuracy.

# Think About It

1. How many significant figures are in 0.02030?
2. Why are leading zeros ignored?

# Summary

Significant figures indicate the precision of a measurement, not just its value.

# Retrieval Practice

1. Count the significant figures in five given numbers.
2. Round 5.67891 to three significant figures.
