# Lesson 06: Measurements

> **Estimated Review Time:** 10–15 minutes

# Learning Objectives

- Understand the importance of measurement in chemistry.
- Differentiate accuracy and precision.
- Recognize measurement uncertainty.
- Choose appropriate measuring instruments.

# Why This Topic Matters

Chemistry is an experimental science. Reliable conclusions depend on reliable measurements.

# Building the Intuition

No measurement is perfectly exact.

Whenever you measure mass, length, volume, or temperature, there is always some uncertainty.

Learning to measure correctly is as important as learning chemical concepts.

# What is Measurement?

Measurement is the process of comparing an unknown quantity with an accepted standard.

Examples:
- Mass → balance
- Length → ruler
- Volume → measuring cylinder
- Temperature → thermometer

# Accuracy vs Precision

**Accuracy**
How close a measurement is to the true value.

**Precision**
How close repeated measurements are to one another.

A set of measurements may be:
- Accurate and precise
- Accurate but not precise
- Precise but not accurate
- Neither

# Measurement Uncertainty

Every instrument has limitations.

Example:
A ruler marked in millimetres cannot reliably measure to the nearest micrometre.

Always record measurements with the correct number of digits.

# Common Sources of Error

- Instrument limitations
- Parallax error
- Incorrect calibration
- Human error

# Common Misconceptions

- Accurate and precise mean the same thing. (They do not.)
- Digital instruments eliminate all errors. (They do not.)

# Think About It

1. Can repeated measurements be precise but inaccurate?
2. Why should you estimate one digit beyond the smallest marking?

# Summary

Good chemistry depends on careful measurement, understanding uncertainty, and distinguishing accuracy from precision.

# Retrieval Practice

1. Define measurement.
2. Differentiate accuracy and precision.
3. List three common measurement errors.
