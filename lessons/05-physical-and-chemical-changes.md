# Lesson 05: Physical and Chemical Changes

> **Estimated Review Time:** 10–15 minutes

# Learning Objectives

- Distinguish between physical and chemical changes.
- Recognize evidence of a chemical change.
- Explain why some changes are reversible and others are not.

# Why This Topic Matters

Every day, matter changes. Ice melts, water boils, iron rusts, wood burns, and food cooks.
Some changes only affect the appearance or state of a substance, while others create entirely new substances.
Understanding this distinction is fundamental to chemistry.

# Building the Intuition

Ask one simple question:

> **Has a new substance been formed?**

- If **no**, it is usually a **physical change**.
- If **yes**, it is a **chemical change**.

# Physical Changes

A physical change alters the form, size, or state of a substance without changing its chemical composition.

Examples:
- Melting ice
- Boiling water
- Breaking glass
- Cutting paper
- Dissolving sugar in water

# Chemical Changes

A chemical change produces one or more new substances with different properties.

Examples:
- Rusting of iron
- Burning wood
- Cooking food
- Digestion
- Souring of milk

# Indicators of a Chemical Change

- Gas produced
- Colour change
- Heat or light released/absorbed
- Formation of a precipitate
- New smell

# Physical vs Chemical Change

| Physical Change | Chemical Change |
|---|---|
| No new substance | New substance formed |
| Usually reversible | Often difficult to reverse |
| Composition unchanged | Composition changes |

# Common Misconceptions

- Melting wax is a physical change.
- Burning wax is a chemical change.
- Dissolving salt in water is usually a physical change.

# Think About It

1. Why is boiling water not a chemical change?
2. Why is rusting irreversible under ordinary conditions?
3. Is crushing chalk a physical or chemical change?

# Summary

The key difference is whether a new substance forms.

# Retrieval Practice

1. Define physical change.
2. Define chemical change.
3. Give five examples of each.
4. List three signs of a chemical change.
