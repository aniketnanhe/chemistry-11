# Lesson 09: Scientific Notation

> **Estimated Review Time:** 10–15 minutes

# Learning Objectives
- Express very large and very small numbers using scientific notation.
- Convert between decimal and scientific notation.
- Perform basic calculations using scientific notation.

# Why This Topic Matters

Chemistry frequently deals with enormous numbers (atoms) and tiny numbers (atomic masses). Scientific notation makes these values easier to read, compare, and calculate.

# Building the Intuition

Instead of writing:

602000000000000000000000

we write:

6.02 × 10^23

Instead of:

0.00000000000000000000000166

we write:

1.66 × 10^-24

# Rules

- Scientific notation has the form a × 10ⁿ.
- 1 ≤ a < 10.
- n is positive for large numbers and negative for small numbers.

# Examples

- 4500 = 4.5 × 10³
- 0.00045 = 4.5 × 10⁻⁴
- 78,900 = 7.89 × 10⁴

# Common Misconceptions

- The coefficient must always be between 1 and 10.
- Moving the decimal right gives a negative exponent.
- Moving the decimal left gives a positive exponent.
